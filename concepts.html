Concepts

Closed-ended vaults, their three phases, first-loss capital, cash-basis accounting, and the two levers the vault owner holds over investor money.

Orma measures credit risk in XLS-65 vaults and XLS-66 lending on the XRP Ledger. This page explains the ledger mechanics the rest of the documentation assumes. It is written for a reader who understands credit funds and does not yet know XRPL.

Everything below was measured on Devnet during the De Vinci Blockchain XRPL Lending Protocol Hackathon in Paris, 12 to 13 September 2026, against rippled 3.4.0-rc5 at wss://s.devnet.rippletest.net:51233. The lending amendments are not live on Mainnet, so every transaction hash on this page resolves on Devnet only.

The vault is a ledger object, not a contract

A vault is a native object on the XRP Ledger. There is no contract code to read. Depositors send VaultDeposit, receive units of a share token, and the object carries the fund's balance sheet in a handful of fields:

FieldMeaning
AssetsTotalEverything the vault counts as an asset, cash plus loans at book
AssetsAvailableThe part not currently lent out
LossUnrealizedLoss the manager has recognised but not yet written off
MPTokenIssuance.OutstandingAmountUnits outstanding. There is no SharesTotal field on the Vault
OwnerThe account that created the vault and controls it
AccountThe vault's pseudo-account, which actually holds the funds

Two of those deserve attention immediately. AssetsTotal does not net LossUnrealized, and units outstanding live on a different ledger object entirely, the share token's MPTokenIssuance. A tool that reads AssetsTotal and divides by units is reading the fund before provisions.

Warning

rippled omits any field whose value equals the type default. A vault with no recognised loss has no

LossUnrealized key at all, not "0". Absent means zero everywhere, and confusing "absent because

zero" with "absent because deleted" is a real source of wrong numbers. See src/num.mjs.

Open-ended and closed-ended

An open-ended vault has no calendar. Deposits and withdrawals are legal at any time, subject to liquidity. It cannot be used for lending: attaching a LoanBroker to one returns tecNO_PERMISSION.

A closed-ended vault is created with VaultKind: 1 plus a SubscriptionDate and a RedemptionDate. It is a fixed-term fund: capital is raised, deployed, then returned.

Note

VaultKind is immutable. A vault created open-ended can never become a lending vault. If you plan

to lend, you decide that at VaultCreate and never again.

The three phases

Phase is derived, not stored. It is a comparison between the validated ledger's close_time and the two dates on the object. This is the whole of it, from src/xrpl.mjs:

export function vaultPhase(vault, closeTime) {
  if (Number(vault.VaultKind ?? 0) !== 1) return PHASE.OPEN
  const sub = Number(vault.SubscriptionDate ?? 0)
  const red = Number(vault.RedemptionDate ?? 0)
  if (closeTime < sub) return PHASE.SUBSCRIPTION
  if (closeTime < red) return PHASE.INVESTMENT
  return PHASE.REDEMPTION
}

The clock is the validated ledger close time, not wall clock. Devnet close time lags wall clock by up to ten seconds, and a countdown computed against the wrong clock goes negative on stage.

Subscription runs until SubscriptionDate. Capital comes in and may leave freely. No lending is possible yet.

Investment runs from SubscriptionDate to RedemptionDate. The book is locked. Loans are originated here, and they are impaired and defaulted here. Investors cannot deposit and cannot withdraw for the entire phase.

Redemption begins at RedemptionDate. Withdrawals reopen, deposits stay closed, and no new loan can be written. Under the default WithdrawalPolicy: 1, redemption is first come first served, so the order investors queue in matters when liquidity is short.

Read the codes as a sentence about time rather than about permission. Deposits are early-only, so after the window they are tecEXPIRED. Withdrawals are bracketed, so inside the lockup they are tecTOO_SOON and after redemption opens they succeed. LoanSet is mid-only, so it is tecTOO_SOON before and tecEXPIRED after.

The one row that did not do what we expected is LoanSet in Investment (A926030097C2AD2F8ED4D1D6899200B27D2C75DF200A5A2EBEA8EBF88339F63E). It is legal there, and three of our four live facilities have loans written in Investment. The fourth, Thorne Senior Secured I, is still in Subscription and carries no loans. In this particular matrix run the loan's scheduled maturity, PaymentInterval 60 seconds times PaymentTotal 2, plus the 60 second kLoanRedemptionBuffer that LoanSet.cpp requires on top of it, overran the vault's remaining RedemptionDate window, and rippled refused it. The rule is StartDate + PaymentInterval × PaymentTotal + 60 ≤ RedemptionDate. GracePeriod is not part of it.

Warning

tecNO_PERMISSION on LoanSet is overloaded. It is returned both for "this vault is not

closed-ended" and for "this loan matures after the vault's RedemptionDate". We confirmed this by

holding everything constant except the term: a 180 second loan succeeded and a 1200 second loan on

the same vault and broker returned tecNO_PERMISSION. Script: src/dev/check-loanset-overload.mjs.

canWithdraw: false is a protocol fact

The single most misread state in this system. During Investment, VaultWithdraw returns tecTOO_SOON for the entire phase, regardless of how much cash the vault is holding. We measured it with 80 XRP of AssetsAvailable sitting idle. It is a phase predicate, not a liquidity check.

// src/poll.mjs
canWithdraw: !inInvestment,
withdrawBlockedReason: inInvestment ? 'tecTOO_SOON' : null,

Orma reports it as a fact with a reason attached, never as an error. From GET /api/vaults/{vaultId} on Calder Structured Credit III:

  "phase": "Investment",
  "canDeposit": false,
  "canWithdraw": false,
  "withdrawBlockedReason": "tecTOO_SOON",
  "nextBoundaryAt": "2026-09-13T15:11:47Z",

Note

Because the exit is closed for the whole of Investment, a credit signal published during Investment

cannot be acted on by selling. That is why the redemption cliff anchors the composite grade rather

than sitting as one factor among five, and why Orma publishes where a third party can act on the

signal instead of only displaying it. See the scoring method.

The LoanBroker and first-loss capital

Loans are not made by the vault. They are made through a LoanBroker, a separate ledger object that points at the vault. The broker posts cover: its own capital, held on the broker's pseudo-account, that absorbs losses before the vault's assets do. This is first-loss capital in the ordinary structured credit sense, and it is the only thing standing between a borrower default and the depositors.

FieldMeaning
DebtTotalPrincipal currently drawn across all loans under this broker
DebtMaximumCeiling on that
CoverAvailableFirst-loss capital posted and not yet consumed
CoverRateMinimumCover the broker must hold, as a fraction of DebtTotal
CoverRateLiquidationFraction of that cover which is actually liquidated on a default

Both rates are expressed in units of $10^{-5}$. A stated 10000 means $0.10$, which is ten percent. Both are divided by $10^5$, and that double product is the detail that makes the cover formula look a hundred times larger than it is. Cover consumed on a single default is

$$T(p) = \min\Big(\big\lceil D \cdot c_{\min} \cdot c_{\text{liq}} \big\rceil,\; p,\; C\Big)$$

where $D$ is DebtTotal, $p$ the principal of the loan that defaulted, and $C$ is CoverAvailable. At $c_{\min} = c_{\text{liq}} = 10000$ the coefficient is $0.10 \times 0.10 = 0.01$. One percent of the book, not ten.

Note what the base is. The formula is keyed to the broker's total book $D$, not to the principal of the loan that failed. A broker with a shrinking book liquidates less cover on each successive default, and a single loan that is most of the book liquidates cover keyed to that whole book once and then leaves nothing behind it. Calder Structured Credit III shows the scale of the gap: 10 XRP of debt, 5 XRP of cover posted, and 0.1 XRP of that cover reachable on a default.

"broker": {
  "debtTotal": "10000000",
  "coverAvailable": "5000000",
  "coverRateMinimum": 10000,
  "coverRateLiquidation": 10000,
  "coverRequired": "1000000",
  "maxLiquidatableNow": "100000",
  "strandedCoverFraction": "0.9800"
}

98 percent of the posted cover cannot be reached by any single default at the current book. Orma reports that as strandedCoverFraction rather than reporting CoverAvailable as though all of it were loss-absorbing.

The broker owner is the vault owner

This is a protocol fact, not a configuration choice. LoanBrokerSet requires the submitting account to be the vault's Owner (LoanBrokerSet.cpp:109), so the party who posts first-loss capital and the party who manages the fund are necessarily the same account. On Calder both read rGMo2ke9of3GEqJwWAzDmFjrm6SwYowTsB.

Only that owner may impair, unimpair or default a loan (LoanManage.cpp:124-129). There is no independent servicer, no trustee, no rating committee.

Warning

Delegation does not fix this. XLS-75 permission delegation returns temMALFORMED for the entire

lending transaction suite, so the credit decisions cannot be handed to another account even if a

manager wanted to.

The consequence runs through everything else on this page. Credit events are declared by the party whose own capital those declarations consume.

Cash-basis accounting, LEVersion = 1

Under LEVersion: 1 the vault keeps cash-basis books. The practical consequences:

  • Origination moves DebtTotal, not AssetsTotal. Lending out cash is not a gain or a loss,

and the vault's asset total does not move when a loan is written.

  • Impairment moves only LossUnrealized. Nothing else on the Vault object changes.
  • Default moves assets. The loss is realised: the asset is written off, the provision is

released, cover is liquidated, and the book reads clean afterwards.

An impairment is a disclosure. It says "this exposure is in trouble" without moving a single asset. A default is a write-off. Both are the vault owner's unilateral decision, and neither has a deadline attached.

Realisation is also why a clean-looking book is not evidence of a clean history. Kestrel Bridge Financing II took 50 XRP of subscriptions, defaulted on 40 XRP, and now holds 10.5 XRP with lossUnrealized of "0" and a NAV divergence of 0 basis points. Nothing in the current object records the loss. Its naive and correct unit values agree at 0.210000, and only the 79 percent gap between units outstanding and assets held says what happened.

Lever one: when to recognise

The manager chooses the moment LossUnrealized moves. Until they act, AssetsTotal carries the troubled loan at book and a naive reader sees a fund at par.

$$\text{NAV}_{\text{naive}} = \frac{A}{S}, \qquad \text{NAV}_{\text{held}} = \frac{A - L}{S}$$

Meridian Trade Finance I, measured live: $A = 51{,}000{,}000$ drops, $L = 10{,}000{,}000$, $S = 51{,}000{,}000$. Naive reads $1.000000$ and held reads $0.803922$, a divergence of 1961 basis points. Same object, same instant, two answers.

The delay is not merely invisible to careless tools. It is invisible to careful ones that watch transaction metadata. rippled omits from PreviousFields any field whose previous value was the type default. On the first impairment of a healthy vault, LossUnrealized moves from zero, and under cash basis nothing else on the Vault changes, so PreviousFields is emitted empty and an indexer that diffs metadata sees no change at all on the most important credit event the protocol has. Captured in transaction 075FE6D2E0F29919AF477A2A8F581A680805A006138BB967D8434611E49229C3, result tesSUCCESS; the verbatim metadata node and the three-outcome asymmetry that lets it survive casual testing are in /findings.

Tip

This is why Orma is a four second poller that re-reads full object state, and never a metadata

differ. Re-reading cannot miss a field that was never diffed. See src/poll.mjs, and

the API reference for the reading it publishes.

Delay also flatters the manager directly. CoverRateMinimum is keyed to DebtTotal, so the cover they must hold, and the cover actually liquidated when they finally act, both shrink with the book.

Lever two: in what order to realise

Because $T$ is keyed to DebtTotal at the instant of the default, and DebtTotal is decremented as each default lands, every default shrinks the base for the next one. Total cover consumed across a fixed set of defaults therefore depends on the order they are declared in.

With $c = c_{\min} c_{\text{liq}}$, $D_0$ the book at the first default, and $k$ defaults in order $\sigma$:

$$T(\sigma, k) = c \left[ k D_0 - \sum_{i=1}^{k} (k - i)\, p_{\sigma(i)} \right]$$

Only the sum depends on $\sigma$, and its coefficient $(k-i)$ decreases in $i$. By the rearrangement inequality the sum is largest, and therefore $T$ smallest, when the principals are declared in decreasing order.

Total first-loss capital consumed is minimised by declaring the largest exposure first, and

maximised by declaring the smallest first.

Kestrel Bridge Financing II did exactly that on Devnet, with two loans of 30 and 10 XRP at ten percent and ten percent, reconstructed from public history alone:

DeclaredPrincipalDebtTotal beforeCover consumed
2026-09-12T21:11:02Z30 XRP40 XRP0.40 XRP
2026-09-12T21:11:10Z10 XRP10 XRP0.10 XRP
total0.50 XRP

Declaring the small loan first would have consumed 0.70 XRP. Identical losses, a 40 percent difference in what the first-loss capital absorbed, and the 0.20 XRP difference falls on the unit holders. Orma places the observed sequence between the two extremes as a sequence score:

$$\phi = \frac{T_{\text{observed}} - T_{\min}}{T_{\max} - T_{\min}}$$

$\phi = 0$ is the investor-worst sequence and $\phi = 1$ the investor-best. Kestrel scored $\phi = 0.0000$. Reconstruction and scoring live in src/history.mjs, served at GET /api/vaults/{vaultId}/broker-history.

One asymmetry, two levers, then enforcement

The vault owner and the broker owner are the same account. That single asymmetry gives one party two levers over investor money: when a loss is recognised, and in what order losses are realised. Both are discretionary, both move money, and neither leaves a signal that a metadata-diffing tool would see.

Orma does three things about it, each on its own page:

  • Measure both levers from public ledger state, with no privileged access. See /mathematics.
  • Publish the result where it can be acted on: a native XLS-47 Oracle object carrying six

dimensions, plus a pointer inside the share token's own metadata. See /mathematics and /nav-pointer.

  • Enforce it with XLS-70 credentials and a permissioned domain, so an independent rater decides

who may subscribe. See /gate.
