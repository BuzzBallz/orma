---

Every ledger figure on this page was read from the running API at ledger `5263343`,
`2026-09-12T22:20:22Z`, against Devnet `rippled 3.4.0-rc5`. Nothing here is a projection.
The two haircut figures in case 2 are arithmetic on that reading rather than a captured
response body, and case 2 says so where they appear.

The thesis the four cases share: the party who decides **when** a vault recognises a loss
is the same party who decides **in what order** those losses are realised. The
`LoanBroker` owner is necessarily the vault owner, a protocol fact and not a configuration
choice. Both decisions move money from unit holders to that party. Orma measures both from
public ledger state, publishes the result where it can be acted on, and lets a third party
enforce it.

<Warning>
All four facilities run on Devnet. XLS-65 (vaults) and XLS-66 (lending) are not live on
Mainnet, so none of this can be pointed at a production facility today. XLS-70
(credentials) and XLS-80 (permissioned domains), which case 4 is built from, **are** live
on Mainnet; what keeps case 4 Devnet-only is the vault underneath it. Every transaction
hash below resolves on `devnet.xrpl.org`.
</Warning>

## 1. An LP choosing between two facilities

Two closed-ended XRP facilities, both in the Investment phase, both scored by the same
code path with no weights applied anywhere.

| | Kestrel Bridge Financing II | Calder Structured Credit III |
|---|---|---|
| `vaultId` | `24EAA01A...E5810089` | `5763707D...A96CB0E5` |
| Vault grade | **B** (26 / 100) | **AA** (89 / 100) |
| Conduct grade | **E** (25 / 100) | **A** (100 / 100) |
| `assetsTotal` | `10500000` drops | `51000000` drops |
| `lossUnrealized` | `0` | `10000000` drops |
| `navNaive` | `0.210000` | `1.000000` |
| `navCorrect` | `0.210000` | `0.803922` |
| `navDivergenceBps` | **0** | **1961** |
| Defaults on record | 2 | 0 |
| Impairments on record | 0 | 1 |

The two columns disagree with each other in exactly the way that matters.

Kestrel reports **zero** NAV divergence. Calder reports 19.61%. A screener that ranks by
divergence puts Kestrel on top. Kestrel is the worse facility by a wide margin, and the
reason is mechanical: divergence only becomes non-zero once a manager has **declared** a
loss. Kestrel's manager never declared one. They went straight to default, at which point
the loss was realised, `LossUnrealized` never moved off zero, and the book read clean
again. This is why `GET /api/vaults` sorts by `gradeNumeric` ascending and not by
divergence.

### What Kestrel's manager actually did

Reconstructed from three transactions on the public record, via
`GET /api/vaults/24EAA01A.../broker-history`:

| event | time | hash | principal | `debtBefore` | cover consumed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cover_deposit` | 21:08:32Z | `E979EB6D...` | `0` | `0` | `0` |
| `default` | 21:11:02Z | `FB23EC53...` | `30000000` | `40000000` | `400000` |
| `default` | 21:11:10Z | `DE49DF0D...` | `10000000` | `10000000` | `100000` |

The 30 XRP loan was declared first, then the 10 XRP loan. Cover liquidated on a default is

$$T(p) = \min\Big(\big\lceil D \cdot c_{\min} \cdot c_{\text{liq}} \big\rceil,\; p,\; C\Big)$$

with $D$ the broker's **total** book, not the defaulting loan's principal, and $D$
decremented after each default. At $c_{\min} = c_{\text{liq}} = 10000$ (both in $10^{-5}$
units, so the coefficient is $0.10 \times 0.10 = 0.01$) the two orders give:

| order | step 1 | step 2 | total cover consumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| largest first (what happened) | `400000` | `100000` | **`500000`** |
| smallest first | `400000` | `300000` | **`700000`** |

The manager's own first-loss capital absorbed 0.50 XRP instead of 0.70 XRP. The 0.20 XRP
difference did not disappear. It fell on the unit holders. The API reports it as
`ordering.costToDepositors: "200000"` with `fairness: "0.0000"`, the sequence sitting
exactly at the investor-worst end of the range.

Conduct score: 100, minus 35 for a sequence score below 0.25, minus 20 for each of two
exposures written off with no prior impairment, giving **25 and a grade of E**.

The facility now holds 10.5 XRP against 50,000,000 units outstanding. Realised capital
destruction is

$$\rho = \max\left(0,\; \frac{S - A}{S}\right) = \frac{50{,}000{,}000 - 10{,}500{,}000}{50{,}000{,}000} = 79\%$$

which notches the composite down 14 steps from its AAA anchor to **B**. Without that term
the vault scores AAA, because after a write-off the asset is gone, the provision is
released and every current-exposure factor reads clean.

### What Calder's manager actually did

One loan, 10 XRP, went past due. The manager impaired it
(`BD18EFB94490CA540E7A9C249971740C1228D1FBC3ED97C9BA9F4908F52D5F4F`), which moved
`LossUnrealized` from `0` to `10000000` and took the honest unit value from 1.000000 down
to 0.803922. No default has been declared. The ordering rule needs two defaults before it
applies, no exposure was written off without warning, and no first-loss capital has been
withdrawn, so nothing deducts from the starting 100 and the conduct grade is **A**.

The vault itself is graded **AA**, not AAA, and the notch trace says why:

```json
"notchTrace": [
  { "from": "AAA", "rule": "anchor on DEADLINE (can claims be met at redemption)", "delta": 0, "to": "AAA" },
  { "from": "AAA", "rule": "single loan >= 75% of broker debt (cover is keyed to total debt, not loan size)", "delta": -1, "to": "AA+" },
  { "from": "AA+", "rule": "liquidatable cover < 5% of recognised loss", "delta": -1, "to": "AA" }
]
```

Both notches are about structure, not about the disclosure. Disclosing the loss cost
Calder 1961 bp on its published unit value and cost its conduct grade nothing.

### Who this is for

An LP with capital to allocate and no way to tell a manager who has taken a loss from a
manager who has hidden one. `AssetsTotal` is the field every generic tool reads, and it
answers neither question. Calder reads `51000000` against 51,000,000 units outstanding, a
naive unit value of exactly `1.000000`, with the 10 XRP loss its manager has already
recognised nowhere in the number. Kestrel reads a divergence of zero because its manager
skipped the recognition step altogether. On conduct, which is the record of what each
manager did, the two are four grades apart: E against A.

```bash
# every tracked facility, worst grade first
curl -s localhost:8787/api/vaults \
  | jq '.vaults[] | {label, grade, gradeNumeric, navDivergenceBps}'

# the manager's record, reconstructed from account_tx
curl -s localhost:8787/api/vaults/24EAA01AD4CE70D8ABB4ACB4255DC4C216B8AF3B9E7315DB8052BFC1E5810089/broker-history \
  | jq '{ordering, reputation}'
```

<Note>
Conduct is reconstructed from `account_tx`, which Devnet retains for roughly 29 days. The
API returns that caveat in the response body rather than burying it in a footnote. Conduct
before the retention window is not visible and is not claimed to be.
</Note>

## 2. A second broker asked to lend against pledged units

An LP holds units of Calder's share token and wants to borrow against them from a
different broker. The units are escrowed on-ledger: XLS-85 `TokenEscrow` accepts MPT
amounts, and vault shares are MPTs, so `EscrowCreate` with
`Amount: { mpt_issuance_id, value }` returns `tesSUCCESS`. The pledge is then a public
object. What it is worth is the open question.

The second broker starts from the only identifier they have, the issuance id, and runs the
resolution loop. Every step is on the ledger or on a URL the ledger names:

```bash
curl -s "localhost:8787/api/mpt/000000014D667775372D5B78E07FFF294678C7F9CE82AFBC/resolve?units=1000000"
```

```json
"steps": [
  { "step": "read MPTokenIssuance", "ok": true, "detail": "issuer r3hE8HanpccSZdgmeHCfYFEkwxFjdDmmvt, 51000000 units outstanding" },
  { "step": "decode metadata", "ok": true, "detail": "valid JSON" },
  { "step": "XLS-89 conformance", "ok": true, "detail": "asset_class rwa" },
  { "step": "find valuation pointer", "ok": true, "detail": "http://localhost:8787/api/mpt/000000014D667775372D5B78E07FFF294678C7F9CE82AFBC/nav" },
  { "step": "follow pointer", "ok": true, "detail": "unit value 0.803922" }
]
```

Nothing in that loop required the borrower's cooperation, and nothing required a
relationship with Orma. The pointer template
`http://localhost:8787/api/mpt/{mpt_issuance_id}/nav` was written into `MPTokenMetadata`
at `VaultCreate`. The broker substitutes the token's own id and follows it.

### The number

A pledge of 1,000,000 units, priced both ways. Returned as integer strings in the vault's
asset base unit, drops for an XRP vault:

```json
"pledge": {
  "units": "1000000",
  "valueHeld": "803922",
  "valueReported": "1000000",
  "overstatement": "196078"
}
```

In XRP: reported 1.000000, held 0.803922, **overstatement 0.196078, or 19.61%**. The
lender who prices on the reported figure is over-collateralised on paper by 0.196078 XRP
per 1,000,000 units, and short by that amount in reality.

### Why a haircut does not save you

A haircut applies to the **held** value, never to the reported one:

$$V_{\text{lendable}} = V_{\text{held}} \cdot (1 - h)$$

At $h = 0.20$ on this pledge, `maxLendable` is `643138` drops. A lender applying the same
20% to the reported value lends `800000` drops against collateral genuinely worth
`803922`. They intended a 20% cushion. They have 3,922 drops of it, 0.49% of the true
value. The haircut was consumed entirely by the misstatement before it absorbed a single
tick of volatility.

No escrow of Calder units was outstanding at the capture above, so `/collateral` returned
`pledgeCount: 0`. Those last two figures are the arithmetic the route performs on the
valuation, not a captured response body.

The diagnostic reports every step including failure, because "this collateral is opaque"
is a legitimate answer to give a lender. An early version of the pointer resolved to the
diagnostic rather than to the valuation, returned a perfectly good HTTP 200 with no
`unitValue` on it, and crashed the caller. `valuePledge` now returns an explicit
`unpriced` string instead of throwing.

```bash
# a lender's own escrows, valued both ways, with their policy discount applied to the honest value
curl -s "localhost:8787/api/vaults/5763707D11EA19D1B5FF04E4EBA4F9336057955CDE65B725D1FF3EF2A96CB0E5/collateral?haircut=20&accounts=rEXAMPLE" \
  | jq '{navNaive, navCorrect, totalOverstatement, totalOverstatementPct, pledges}'
```

### Who this is for

The second broker, whose alternative is to price the collateral on the borrower's word or
to refuse it outright. The first option costs 19.61% of the stated notional on this
facility. The second costs the borrower their access to credit, and costs the vault the
argument that its units are collateral at all.

<Warning>
No global index exists from an `mpt_issuance_id` to the escrows holding it, so the
`/collateral` route has to be handed a list of accounts to scan. An escrow does appear in
the owner directory of both pledgor and beneficiary with the same object index, so a
lender can enumerate pledges made in their own favour by scanning their own objects, which
is the question that actually matters. We dedupe by index because the same escrow comes
back once per account scanned. This is filed as developer feedback, not solved.
</Warning>

## 3. A vault manager who wants to attract capital

Being measured looks adversarial and is not. The chain is short:

1. The share token carries a pointer to an honest valuation.
2. Any counterparty can price the units without asking the holder, the manager, or us.
3. Units that can be priced are usable as collateral elsewhere.
4. Units that are usable elsewhere are worth more to hold.
5. A facility whose units are worth more to hold raises capital more easily.

Calder's manager did step 1 by writing 502 bytes of JSON into `MPTokenMetadata` at
`VaultCreate`. Five XLS-89 fields so explorers will list the token, then one namespaced
block that does the work:

```json
"orma": {
  "v": 1,
  "instrument": "vault-share",
  "nav_url": "http://localhost:8787/api/mpt/{mpt_issuance_id}/nav",
  "nav_url_param": "{mpt_issuance_id}",
  "nav_basis": "assets net of recognised loss, divided by units outstanding",
  "doc": "https://orma.credit/nav"
}
```

<Warning>
This is write-once, permanently: no transaction and no key can update it, including the
manager's and including ours. That is why the field holds a **pointer** and never a number,
and it is why the hackathon build shipped a `localhost` URL into the metadata of a live
Devnet token, which we cannot correct and are stating rather than hiding. The full object,
the three transactions that establish the immutability, and the 1024-byte ceiling are on
[the NAV pointer page](/nav-pointer).
</Warning>

### The grade is the asset, not the threat

Calder's manager holds a conduct score of **100 / 100, grade A**, earned by impairing a
loan rather than sitting on it. That grade has three properties an honest manager wants:

- **It is not self-reported.** It is rebuilt from transaction hashes anyone can fetch. A
  competitor cannot claim it, and a manager cannot be talked out of it.
- **Disclosure does not cost it.** Realised capital destruction is measured against $A$
  and deliberately not against $A - L$. An unrealised loss is a provision against an asset
  the vault still holds and may recover. Counting it would punish a manager for disclosing
  early, which is the behaviour the rest of the system exists to reward. Kestrel, which
  disclosed nothing, carries 79% realised destruction, a vault grade of B and a conduct
  grade of E. Similarly, first-loss adequacy divides by
  $\max(L, P_{\text{distressed}})$ rather than by $L$ alone, so concealing a large overdue
  exposure cannot improve a score.
- **It is the passport to gated capital.** See case 4. An allocator who deposits only
  where a trusted issuer has certified the counterparties is an allocator a grade A
  manager can reach and a grade E manager cannot.

The measurement also hands the manager something they can use. When two or more distressed
exposures are outstanding, `recommendation` in the broker-history response names the
declaration order that applies the most first-loss capital on the investors' behalf, with
`coverIfFair`, `coverIfSelfServing` and `atStakeForDepositors`. On Calder it currently
returns `applicable: false, reason: "fewer than two distressed loans"`, which is the right
answer rather than a fabricated one.

### Who this is for

A manager running a facility on fundamentals, who is currently priced identically to one
who is not, because `AssetsTotal` is the field every generic tool reads and recognising a
loss does not move it. Under cash-basis accounting an impairment changes nothing else on
the Vault object either, so the disclosure is invisible to anything reading that field
alone.

## 4. A protocol or treasury gating capital

Everything above is measurement, and measurement is advice. This is the part that binds.
The ledger refuses the transaction. Nobody has to read a report, agree with it, or act on
it.

Thorne Senior Secured I, `4A5A8E37...B00190DF5`, verified end to end on Devnet:

| step | transaction | result | hash |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `CredentialCreate`, we issue `ORMA-IG` to an LP | `tesSUCCESS` | `619CC767...` |
| 2 | `CredentialAccept`, the LP opts in | `tesSUCCESS` | `41F98989...` |
| 3 | `PermissionedDomainSet`, the vault owner names our issuer | `tesSUCCESS` | `4C425830...` |
| 4 | `VaultCreate` with `tfVaultPrivate` and `DomainID` | `tesSUCCESS` | `3FC79BF7...` |
| 5 | `VaultDeposit`, graded LP | `tesSUCCESS` | `7FC5C01B...` |
| 6 | `VaultDeposit`, ungraded LP | **`tecNO_AUTH`** | `E7CF5DB2...` |
| 7 | `CredentialDelete`, we revoke | `tesSUCCESS` | `E2D6F396...` |
| 8 | `VaultDeposit` after revocation | **`tecNO_AUTH`** | `AA25246A...` |
| 9 | `VaultWithdraw` after revocation | `tesSUCCESS` | `2E71A259...` |

Resulting state, from `GET /api/vaults/4A5A8E37.../gate`:

```json
{
  "gated": true,
  "private": true,
  "domainId": "FFBEC89D98B4E7CF52F4F254235086514A90CED0EC531941BDF747AF40C5A2FB",
  "acceptedCredentials": [
    { "issuer": "rKQjjU5KFs9RAZCDvYVjcaoVK5gGsCJgkP", "type": "ORMA-IG", "typeHex": "4F524D412D4947" }
  ],
  "domainOwner": "rGFFSqqY1R3764FKF7crU5F6bYh9qaD45S",
  "issuerNamed": true,
  "raterAddress": "rKQjjU5KFs9RAZCDvYVjcaoVK5gGsCJgkP"
}
```

### Why this is a rating and not a private arrangement

Step 3 is signed by the **vault owner alone**. We sign nothing, we are not consulted, and
we cannot decline to be cited. `issuerNamed: true` in the response above is us answering a
question about ourselves that we were never asked in the first place. That asymmetry is
the difference between a rating and a bilateral deal, and it is how the incumbent rating
agencies work: an issuer references a rating, and the agency gets no veto over being
referenced.

Step 2 is the mirror asymmetry. Issuance alone grants nothing. Without `CredentialAccept`
the deposit is still `tecNO_AUTH`, so a credential is not something that can be done **to**
someone.

<Note>
Credentials are keyed to the **bar**, not to the facility. `ORMA-IG` says "may enter
facilities graded BBB- or better" and is reusable across every vault that names our
issuer. One credential per facility would be a whitelist wearing a credential's clothes.
`CredentialType` is a hex blob of 1 to 64 bytes; `ORMA-IG` serialises to `4F524D412D4947`.
</Note>

### Revocation is one-directional, on purpose

Steps 8 and 9 are the sharpest pair in the sequence. After `CredentialDelete`, the LP's
next deposit bounces immediately and their existing position is untouched: they withdraw
in full, `tesSUCCESS`. The gate controls who may **enter**, never who may leave. The
alternative design is a rater who can trap capital, which nobody should accept and which
we did not build.

### Two things that will cost you a day if you do not know them

<Warning>
`DomainID` is **not** stored on the Vault ledger entry. It lives on the share
`MPTokenIssuance`. Reading the Vault object to find out whether a vault is gated returns
nothing, which reads exactly like "open to everyone". `gateStatus()` reads the issuance.

`tfVaultPrivate` (`0x00010000`) is **required** alongside `DomainID` on `VaultCreate`. A
`DomainID` without the flag gates nothing at all, and the API reports that case explicitly
as `"carries a domain but is not flagged private, so nothing is enforced"` rather than
showing it as protected.
</Warning>

<Tip>
Every gate transaction is submitted and then read back from the **validated** result, not
from `engine_result`. We observed an engine result of `tecNO_AUTH` go on to validate as
`tesSUCCESS`. On this feature specifically, reporting the provisional answer is the
difference between "the ledger refused them" and "the ledger let them in".
</Tip>

### Who this is for

A treasury, DAO or protocol that wants an allocation policy enforced by the ledger rather
than by a spreadsheet and a promise. The policy is expressed once, as a domain naming an
issuer, and every deposit is then checked against it by consensus.

## Limits

- Devnet only, because XLS-65 and XLS-66 are not on Mainnet. XLS-70 credentials and
  XLS-80 permissioned domains are, so the enforcement half of case 4 has a production
  path ahead of the measurement half.
- Conduct history is bounded by `account_tx` retention, roughly 29 days.
- The credential bar is a fixed threshold, `ORMA-IG` at BBB- or better. Nothing
  automatically reissues or revokes when a grade moves.
- The pointer written into Calder's metadata is a `localhost` URL and cannot be changed.
- Grades are advisory. `assessment.advisory` is `true` on every valuation response. The
  only thing that binds is the domain in case 4.

Full field-by-field responses for every route used above, namely `/api/vaults`,
`/api/vaults/{vaultId}/nav`, `/api/vaults/{vaultId}/broker-history`,
`/api/vaults/{vaultId}/collateral`, `/api/vaults/{vaultId}/gate` and
`/api/mpt/{mptId}/resolve`, are in [the API reference](/api-reference).
