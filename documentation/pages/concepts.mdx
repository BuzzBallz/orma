Orma measures credit risk in XLS-65 vaults and XLS-66 lending on the XRP Ledger. This page explains
the ledger mechanics the rest of the documentation assumes. It is written for a reader who
understands credit funds and does not yet know XRPL.

Everything below was measured on Devnet during the De Vinci Blockchain XRPL Lending Protocol
Hackathon in Paris, 12 to 13 September 2026, against rippled `3.4.0-rc5` at
`wss://s.devnet.rippletest.net:51233`. The lending amendments are not live on Mainnet, so every
transaction hash on this page resolves on Devnet only.

## The vault is a ledger object, not a contract

A vault is a native object on the XRP Ledger. There is no contract code to read. Depositors send
`VaultDeposit`, receive units of a share token, and the object carries the fund's balance sheet in a
handful of fields:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `AssetsTotal` | Everything the vault counts as an asset, cash plus loans at book |
| `AssetsAvailable` | The part not currently lent out |
| `LossUnrealized` | Loss the manager has recognised but not yet written off |
| `MPTokenIssuance.OutstandingAmount` | Units outstanding. There is no `SharesTotal` field on the Vault |
| `Owner` | The account that created the vault and controls it |
| `Account` | The vault's pseudo-account, which actually holds the funds |

Two of those deserve attention immediately. `AssetsTotal` does **not** net `LossUnrealized`, and
units outstanding live on a different ledger object entirely, the share token's `MPTokenIssuance`.
A tool that reads `AssetsTotal` and divides by units is reading the fund before provisions.

<Warning>
rippled omits any field whose value equals the type default. A vault with no recognised loss has no
`LossUnrealized` key at all, not `"0"`. Absent means zero everywhere, and confusing "absent because
zero" with "absent because deleted" is a real source of wrong numbers. See `src/num.mjs`.
</Warning>

## Open-ended and closed-ended

An **open-ended** vault has no calendar. Deposits and withdrawals are legal at any time, subject to
liquidity. It cannot be used for lending: attaching a `LoanBroker` to one returns `tecNO_PERMISSION`.

A **closed-ended** vault is created with `VaultKind: 1` plus a `SubscriptionDate` and a
`RedemptionDate`. It is a fixed-term fund: capital is raised, deployed, then returned.

<Note>
`VaultKind` is immutable. A vault created open-ended can never become a lending vault. If you plan
to lend, you decide that at `VaultCreate` and never again.
</Note>

## The three phases

Phase is derived, not stored. It is a comparison between the validated ledger's `close_time` and the
two dates on the object. This is the whole of it, from `src/xrpl.mjs`:

```js
export function vaultPhase(vault, closeTime) {
  if (Number(vault.VaultKind ?? 0) !== 1) return PHASE.OPEN
  const sub = Number(vault.SubscriptionDate ?? 0)
  const red = Number(vault.RedemptionDate ?? 0)
  if (closeTime < sub) return PHASE.SUBSCRIPTION
  if (closeTime < red) return PHASE.INVESTMENT
  return PHASE.REDEMPTION
}
```

The clock is the validated ledger close time, not wall clock. Devnet close time lags wall clock by
up to ten seconds, and a countdown computed against the wrong clock goes negative on stage.

**Subscription** runs until `SubscriptionDate`. Capital comes in and may leave freely. No lending is
possible yet.

**Investment** runs from `SubscriptionDate` to `RedemptionDate`. The book is locked. Loans are
originated here, and they are impaired and defaulted here. Investors cannot deposit and cannot
withdraw for the entire phase.

**Redemption** begins at `RedemptionDate`. Withdrawals reopen, deposits stay closed, and no new loan
can be written. Under the default `WithdrawalPolicy: 1`, redemption is first come first served, so
the order investors queue in matters when liquidity is short.

## The phase rejection matrix

We walked one closed-ended vault through all three phases and attempted `VaultDeposit`,
`VaultWithdraw` and `LoanSet` in each, next to controls that succeed where the action is legal.
Without the controls a rejection proves nothing: it could be a malformed transaction. Script:
`src/demo/phase-matrix.mjs`. Closed-ended vault
`2ABEF788EA69A4E09D2F9F1E3897DEBC7FDBDD8A8902ED3D9613A5C7A11ED6F9`. The three `OpenEnded` rows below
ran against a separate open-ended vault,
`C10D1813DECEFA75B23D9436AE1F9E24406EDB7AF038DF09E088A270036F649C`.

| Phase | Transaction | Expected | Measured result |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenEnded | `VaultDeposit` (control) | succeed | `tesSUCCESS` |
| OpenEnded | `VaultWithdraw` (control) | succeed | `tesSUCCESS` |
| OpenEnded | `LoanBrokerSet` on an open vault | reject | `tecNO_PERMISSION` |
| Subscription | `VaultDeposit` (control) | succeed | `tesSUCCESS` |
| Subscription | `VaultWithdraw` (control) | succeed | `tesSUCCESS` |
| Subscription | `LoanSet` before subscription closes | reject | `tecTOO_SOON` |
| Investment | `VaultDeposit` | reject | `tecEXPIRED` |
| Investment | `VaultWithdraw` | reject | `tecTOO_SOON` |
| Investment | `LoanSet` (control) | succeed | `tecNO_PERMISSION`, see below |
| Redemption | `VaultDeposit` | reject | `tecEXPIRED` |
| Redemption | `VaultWithdraw` (control) | succeed | `tesSUCCESS` |
| Redemption | `LoanSet` after redemption opens | reject | `tecEXPIRED` |

Full transaction hashes for the six rejections, all on Devnet:

```text

tecTOO_SOON       VaultWithdraw, Investment   CC104682C298DFB3619EE4C15F0112ACE2B6A95B4971CA0A0AC748F9B5B6617A
tecEXPIRED        VaultDeposit, Redemption    DEA67094F92657625C795FD34B46DF751F2E54B3ECEA1B686777D5C141CAA670

```

Read the codes as a sentence about time rather than about permission. Deposits are early-only, so
after the window they are `tecEXPIRED`. Withdrawals are bracketed, so inside the lockup they are
`tecTOO_SOON` and after redemption opens they succeed. `LoanSet` is mid-only, so it is `tecTOO_SOON`
before and `tecEXPIRED` after.

The one row that did not do what we expected is `LoanSet` in Investment
(`A926030097C2AD2F8ED4D1D6899200B27D2C75DF200A5A2EBEA8EBF88339F63E`). It is legal there, and three of
our four live facilities have loans written in Investment. The fourth, Thorne Senior Secured I, is
still in Subscription and carries no loans. In this particular matrix run the loan's scheduled
maturity, `PaymentInterval` 60 seconds times `PaymentTotal` 2, plus the 60 second
`kLoanRedemptionBuffer` that `LoanSet.cpp` requires on top of it, overran the vault's remaining
`RedemptionDate` window, and rippled refused it. The rule is
`StartDate + PaymentInterval × PaymentTotal + 60 ≤ RedemptionDate`. `GracePeriod` is not part of it.

<Warning>
`tecNO_PERMISSION` on `LoanSet` is overloaded. It is returned both for "this vault is not
closed-ended" and for "this loan matures after the vault's `RedemptionDate`". We confirmed this by
holding everything constant except the term: a 180 second loan succeeded and a 1200 second loan on
the same vault and broker returned `tecNO_PERMISSION`. Script: `src/dev/check-loanset-overload.mjs`.
</Warning>

## `canWithdraw: false` is a protocol fact

The single most misread state in this system. During Investment, `VaultWithdraw` returns
`tecTOO_SOON` for the entire phase, regardless of how much cash the vault is holding. We measured it
with 80 XRP of `AssetsAvailable` sitting idle. It is a phase predicate, not a liquidity check.

```js
// src/poll.mjs
canWithdraw: !inInvestment,
withdrawBlockedReason: inInvestment ? 'tecTOO_SOON' : null,
```

Orma reports it as a fact with a reason attached, never as an error. From
`GET /api/vaults/{vaultId}` on Calder Structured Credit III:

```json
"phaseInfo": {
  "phase": "Investment",
  "canDeposit": false,
  "canWithdraw": false,
  "withdrawBlockedReason": "tecTOO_SOON",
  "nextBoundaryAt": "2026-09-13T15:11:47Z",
  "secondsToNextBoundary": 60686,
  "claimsAtRedemption": "41000000",
  "liquidityAtRedemption": "41100000",
  "projectedShortfall": "0"
}
```

<Note>
Because the exit is closed for the whole of Investment, a credit signal published during Investment
cannot be acted on by selling. That is why the redemption cliff anchors the composite grade rather
than sitting as one factor among five, and why Orma publishes where a third party can act on the
signal instead of only displaying it. See [the scoring method](/mathematics#4-the-five-measured-factors).
</Note>

## The LoanBroker and first-loss capital

Loans are not made by the vault. They are made through a `LoanBroker`, a separate ledger object that
points at the vault. The broker posts **cover**: its own capital, held on the broker's
pseudo-account, that absorbs losses before the vault's assets do. This is first-loss capital in the
ordinary structured credit sense, and it is the only thing standing between a borrower default and
the depositors.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `DebtTotal` | Principal currently drawn across all loans under this broker |
| `DebtMaximum` | Ceiling on that |
| `CoverAvailable` | First-loss capital posted and not yet consumed |
| `CoverRateMinimum` | Cover the broker must hold, as a fraction of `DebtTotal` |
| `CoverRateLiquidation` | Fraction of that cover which is actually liquidated on a default |

Both rates are expressed in units of $10^{-5}$. A stated `10000` means $0.10$, which is ten percent.
Both are divided by $10^5$, and that **double product** is the detail that makes the cover formula
look a hundred times larger than it is. Cover consumed on a single default is

$$T(p) = \min\Big(\big\lceil D \cdot c_{\min} \cdot c_{\text{liq}} \big\rceil,\; p,\; C\Big)$$

where $D$ is `DebtTotal`, $p$ the principal of the loan that defaulted, and $C$ is `CoverAvailable`.
At $c_{\min} = c_{\text{liq}} = 10000$ the coefficient is $0.10 \times 0.10 = 0.01$. One percent of
the book, not ten.

Note what the base is. The formula is keyed to the broker's **total** book $D$, not to the principal
of the loan that failed. A broker with a shrinking book liquidates less cover on each successive
default, and a single loan that is most of the book liquidates cover keyed to that whole book once
and then leaves nothing behind it. Calder Structured Credit III shows the scale of the gap: 10 XRP
of debt, 5 XRP of cover posted, and 0.1 XRP of that cover reachable on a default.

```json
"broker": {
  "debtTotal": "10000000",
  "coverAvailable": "5000000",
  "coverRateMinimum": 10000,
  "coverRateLiquidation": 10000,
  "coverRequired": "1000000",
  "maxLiquidatableNow": "100000",
  "strandedCoverFraction": "0.9800"
}
```

98 percent of the posted cover cannot be reached by any single default at the current book. Orma
reports that as `strandedCoverFraction` rather than reporting `CoverAvailable` as though all of it
were loss-absorbing.

## The broker owner is the vault owner

This is a protocol fact, not a configuration choice. `LoanBrokerSet` requires the submitting account
to be the vault's `Owner` (`LoanBrokerSet.cpp:109`), so the party who posts first-loss capital and
the party who manages the fund are necessarily the same account. On Calder both read
`rGMo2ke9of3GEqJwWAzDmFjrm6SwYowTsB`.

Only that owner may impair, unimpair or default a loan (`LoanManage.cpp:124-129`). There is no
independent servicer, no trustee, no rating committee.

<Warning>
Delegation does not fix this. XLS-75 permission delegation returns `temMALFORMED` for the entire
lending transaction suite, so the credit decisions cannot be handed to another account even if a
manager wanted to.
</Warning>

The consequence runs through everything else on this page. Credit events are declared by the party
whose own capital those declarations consume.

## Cash-basis accounting, `LEVersion = 1`

Under `LEVersion: 1` the vault keeps cash-basis books. The practical consequences:

- **Origination moves `DebtTotal`, not `AssetsTotal`.** Lending out cash is not a gain or a loss,
  and the vault's asset total does not move when a loan is written.
- **Impairment moves only `LossUnrealized`.** Nothing else on the Vault object changes.
- **Default moves assets.** The loss is realised: the asset is written off, the provision is
  released, cover is liquidated, and the book reads clean afterwards.

### Impairment against default

| | Impairment | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction | `LoanManage` with flag `0x20000` | `LoanManage` with flag `0x10000` |
| Precondition | The loan must already be past due | Past due plus `GracePeriod` elapsed |
| Reversible | Yes, unimpair is flag `0x40000` | No |
| Loan object | Gains flag `131072` | Gains flag `65536`, and rippled **deletes** `PrincipalOutstanding` and `NextPaymentDueDate` |
| `Vault.LossUnrealized` | Increases by the exposure | Released for that exposure |
| `Vault.AssetsTotal` | Unchanged | Decreases by the amount written off |
| `LoanBroker.CoverAvailable` | Unchanged | Decreases by $T(p)$ |
| `LoanBroker.DebtTotal` | Unchanged | Decreases by the principal |

<Note>
Test loan flags by bit, never by equality. A loan can carry more than one, and `Flags === 131072`
silently stops matching the day it does. `src/num.mjs` exposes `hasFlag` for exactly this.
</Note>

An impairment is a disclosure. It says "this exposure is in trouble" without moving a single asset.
A default is a write-off. Both are the vault owner's unilateral decision, and neither has a deadline
attached.

Realisation is also why a clean-looking book is not evidence of a clean history. Kestrel Bridge
Financing II took 50 XRP of subscriptions, defaulted on 40 XRP, and now holds 10.5 XRP with
`lossUnrealized` of `"0"` and a NAV divergence of 0 basis points. Nothing in the current object
records the loss. Its naive and correct unit values agree at `0.210000`, and only the 79 percent gap
between units outstanding and assets held says what happened.

## Lever one: when to recognise

The manager chooses the moment `LossUnrealized` moves. Until they act, `AssetsTotal` carries the
troubled loan at book and a naive reader sees a fund at par.

$$\text{NAV}_{\text{naive}} = \frac{A}{S}, \qquad \text{NAV}_{\text{held}} = \frac{A - L}{S}$$

Meridian Trade Finance I, measured live: $A = 51{,}000{,}000$ drops, $L = 10{,}000{,}000$,
$S = 51{,}000{,}000$. Naive reads $1.000000$ and held reads $0.803922$, a divergence of 1961 basis
points. Same object, same instant, two answers.

The delay is not merely invisible to careless tools. It is invisible to careful ones that watch
transaction metadata. rippled omits from `PreviousFields` any field whose previous value was the
type default. On the first impairment of a healthy vault, `LossUnrealized` moves from zero, and
under cash basis nothing else on the Vault changes, so `PreviousFields` is emitted **empty** and an
indexer that diffs metadata sees no change at all on the most important credit event the protocol
has. Captured in transaction
`075FE6D2E0F29919AF477A2A8F581A680805A006138BB967D8434611E49229C3`, result `tesSUCCESS`; the
verbatim metadata node and the three-outcome asymmetry that lets it survive casual testing are in
[/findings](/findings).

<Tip>
This is why Orma is a four second poller that re-reads full object state, and never a metadata
differ. Re-reading cannot miss a field that was never diffed. See `src/poll.mjs`, and
[the API reference](/api-reference) for the reading it publishes.
</Tip>

Delay also flatters the manager directly. `CoverRateMinimum` is keyed to `DebtTotal`, so the cover
they must hold, and the cover actually liquidated when they finally act, both shrink with the book.

## Lever two: in what order to realise

Because $T$ is keyed to `DebtTotal` at the instant of the default, and `DebtTotal` is decremented as
each default lands, every default shrinks the base for the next one. Total cover consumed across a
fixed set of defaults therefore depends on the **order** they are declared in.

With $c = c_{\min} c_{\text{liq}}$, $D_0$ the book at the first default, and $k$ defaults in order
$\sigma$:

$$T(\sigma, k) = c \left[ k D_0 - \sum_{i=1}^{k} (k - i)\, p_{\sigma(i)} \right]$$

Only the sum depends on $\sigma$, and its coefficient $(k-i)$ decreases in $i$. By the rearrangement
inequality the sum is largest, and therefore $T$ smallest, when the principals are declared in
decreasing order.

> Total first-loss capital consumed is minimised by declaring the largest exposure first, and
> maximised by declaring the smallest first.

Kestrel Bridge Financing II did exactly that on Devnet, with two loans of 30 and 10 XRP at ten
percent and ten percent, reconstructed from public history alone:

| Declared | Principal | `DebtTotal` before | Cover consumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-12T21:11:02Z | 30 XRP | 40 XRP | 0.40 XRP |
| 2026-09-12T21:11:10Z | 10 XRP | 10 XRP | 0.10 XRP |
| | | **total** | **0.50 XRP** |

Declaring the small loan first would have consumed 0.70 XRP. Identical losses, a 40 percent
difference in what the first-loss capital absorbed, and the 0.20 XRP difference falls on the unit
holders. Orma places the observed sequence between the two extremes as a sequence score:

$$\phi = \frac{T_{\text{observed}} - T_{\min}}{T_{\max} - T_{\min}}$$

$\phi = 0$ is the investor-worst sequence and $\phi = 1$ the investor-best. Kestrel scored
$\phi = 0.0000$. Reconstruction and scoring live in `src/history.mjs`, served at
[`GET /api/vaults/{vaultId}/broker-history`](/api-reference).

<Note>
Reconstructing that history needs a join that is not obvious, and getting it wrong inverts the
answer rather than emptying it. The reason, the affected-node set that proves it, and the four lines
that close it are finding 4 in [/findings](/findings).
</Note>

## One asymmetry, two levers, then enforcement

The vault owner and the broker owner are the same account. That single asymmetry gives one party two
levers over investor money: **when** a loss is recognised, and **in what order** losses are realised.
Both are discretionary, both move money, and neither leaves a signal that a metadata-diffing tool
would see.

Orma does three things about it, each on its own page:

- **Measure** both levers from public ledger state, with no privileged access. See [/mathematics](/mathematics).
- **Publish** the result where it can be acted on: a native XLS-47 Oracle object carrying six
  dimensions, plus a pointer inside the share token's own metadata. See
  [/mathematics](/mathematics#8-publication-as-an-oracle-object) and [/nav-pointer](/nav-pointer).
- **Enforce** it with XLS-70 credentials and a permissioned domain, so an independent rater decides
  who may subscribe. See [/gate](/gate).
