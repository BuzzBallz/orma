title: "Orma"
description: "Credit measurement, publication and enforcement for XLS-65 vaults and XLS-66 lending on the XRP Ledger."
---

Orma reads the solvency of an XLS-65 vault from public ledger state, publishes the reading
as a native XLS-47 Oracle object and as a pointer inside the share token's own metadata,
and lets an independent third party turn that reading into an XLS-70 credential that a
permissioned domain enforces at deposit time.

It was built at the De Vinci Blockchain XRPL Lending Protocol Hackathon in Paris, 12 and
13 September 2026, by Alexandre Lemiere (backend, protocol) and Andrea Gonzalez
(frontend). Everything described here runs against XRPL Devnet, `rippled` 3.4.0-rc5,
because the lending amendments are not on Mainnet. XLS-47 is the one component with a
production path today.

## A loss nobody was told about

A closed-ended lending vault called Meridian Trade Finance I takes 51 XRP of subscriptions
and lends 10 XRP through its LoanBroker. The borrower stops paying. Once the loan is past
due the manager impairs it, which is the correct and honest thing to do: it recognises the
loss against the vault before anyone redeems.

Here is the Vault ledger entry before and after, in drops:

| field | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `AssetsTotal` | `51000000` | `51000000` |
| `AssetsAvailable` | `41000000` | `41000000` |
| `LossUnrealized` | `0` | `10000000` |
| `MPTokenIssuance.OutstandingAmount` | `51000000` | `51000000` |

Two readings of the same object. The first divides assets by units:

$$\text{NAV}_{\text{naive}} = \frac{A}{S} = \frac{51{,}000{,}000}{51{,}000{,}000} = 1.000000$$

The second nets out the loss the manager has already recognised:

$$\text{NAV}_{\text{held}} = \frac{A - L}{S} = \frac{51{,}000{,}000 - 10{,}000{,}000}{51{,}000{,}000} = 0.803922$$

The gap is 1961 basis points, measured against the reported figure. A holder who sells,
redeems or pledges a unit at par hands over 1.000000 for a claim on 0.803922, so 19.61% of
the price is backed by nothing the vault holds.

Now the part that makes this more than an arithmetic footnote. The impairment transaction
is `075FE6D2E0F29919AF477A2A8F581A680805A006138BB967D8434611E49229C3`, result
`tesSUCCESS`. Its metadata for the Vault node reads:

```json
{
  "ledgerIndex": "864C5A2DDCED78C6198B0703169D533B747A56E5F9398D832033F79E37E8C835",
  "previousFields": {},
  "finalFields": {
    "AssetsTotal": "51000000",
    "AssetsAvailable": "41000000",
    "LossUnrealized": "10000000"
  }
}
```

`previousFields` is empty. `rippled` omits from `PreviousFields` any field whose previous
value was the type default, and `LossUnrealized` was `0`. Under cash-basis accounting
(`LEVersion = 1`) an impairment touches nothing else on the Vault object: it does not move
`AssetsTotal` and it does not move `AssetsAvailable`. So there is no field left to diff. An
indexer that builds vault state by applying transaction metadata deltas is told nothing at
all, and keeps serving a NAV of 1.000000.

<Warning>
  The first impairment of a previously healthy vault is invisible to metadata diffing.
  Every subsequent one is visible, because `LossUnrealized` is no longer `0`. The single
  event that matters most is the one that disappears.
</Warning>

The associated Loan object does change: its `Flags` go from `0` to `131072`, which is
`lsfLoanImpaired` (`0x20000`). An indexer watching loans would catch it. But the money
lives on the Vault, and it is the Vault node that emits nothing.

Orma's reader therefore never diffs `PreviousFields`. It polls a known list of vault ids
every 4000 ms and re-reads each object in full: the Vault, the LoanBrokers that point at
it, and every Loan under those brokers. Re-reading costs more requests. It cannot miss an
empty diff. See [/findings](/findings).

## One party, two levers

The impairment above was discretionary. Nothing in the protocol obliged the manager to
declare it that day rather than a week later, and `VaultWithdraw` returns `tecTOO_SOON`
for the entire Investment phase, so an investor who suspects a problem cannot leave. That
is lever one: **when** a loss is recognised.

Lever two is **in what order** losses are realised, and it moves real money. When a loan
defaults, the amount of first-loss cover the broker forfeits is

$$T(p) = \min\Big(\big\lceil D \cdot c_{\min} \cdot c_{\text{liq}} \big\rceil,\; p,\; C\Big)$$

where $D$ is the broker's total drawn debt at that moment, not the principal of the loan
that defaulted, and both cover rates are in units of $10^{-5}$, so both are divided by
$10^5$. Because $D$ is decremented as each default lands, declaring a large exposure first
shrinks the base for everything that follows.

Kestrel Bridge Financing II, broker
`8DB104980C31CBE9F196508E7D748E8C31FB783CD4E0A3620AE0B476CBDEA3FA`, with
$c_{\min} = c_{\text{liq}} = 10000$ (0.10 each, so a coefficient of 0.01) and a book of
40 XRP split into a 30 XRP and a 10 XRP exposure:

| order declared | first default | second default | cover consumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 XRP then 10 XRP | $\lceil 40 \cdot 0.01 \rceil = 0.40$ | $\lceil 10 \cdot 0.01 \rceil = 0.10$ | **0.500000 XRP** |
| 10 XRP then 30 XRP | $\lceil 40 \cdot 0.01 \rceil = 0.40$ | $\lceil 30 \cdot 0.01 \rceil = 0.30$ | **0.700000 XRP** |

Same two losses, same day, 0.2 XRP difference in what the manager's own capital absorbed.
The shortfall falls on unit holders. On Devnet the manager declared the 30 XRP exposure
first, in transactions `FB23EC5313F85FA157D56966E0B0B1665068905988D925C70BB0E7C35C3B221B`
and `DE49DF0D6136B96AD28B253CD32100E5715078FD2D0AE6D1DB747AFECB803D1F`, eight seconds
apart. Cover went 10.000000 to 9.600000 to 9.500000 XRP.

This generalises. Writing $c = c_{\min} c_{\text{liq}}$ and $D_0$ for the book at the first
default, declaring $k$ defaults in order $\sigma$ consumes

$$T(\sigma, k) = c \left[ k D_0 - \sum_{i=1}^{k} (k - i)\, p_{\sigma(i)} \right]$$

Only the sum depends on $\sigma$, and $(k-i)$ decreases in $i$, so by the rearrangement
inequality the total is minimised by declaring the largest exposure first. Full derivation
and the three independent agreements that check it in [/mathematics](/mathematics).

The party who chooses the order is the LoanBroker owner. The LoanBroker owner is
necessarily the vault owner: `LoanBrokerSet` returns `tecNO_PERMISSION` otherwise, and
XLS-75 delegation is `temMALFORMED` for the whole lending suite. It is also the party
whose cover is consumed. So the same account decides when the loss is recognised and in
what order it is realised, and both decisions move money from investors to them.

One asymmetry, two levers. Orma's answer is three steps.

## Measure

Orma scores five factors independently on a 20-step ordinal ladder from AAA to D:
liquidity, first-loss adequacy, concentration, recognition lag, and the redemption cliff.
No weights are applied anywhere, because a weighted average lets a strong factor pay for a
broken one. The composite anchors on the redemption cliff and is notched down by rule, and
the largest single notch, up to $-14$, comes from realised capital destruction
$\rho = \max(0, (S - A)/S)$, measured against $A$ and not against $A - L$. A vault that
took 50 XRP, defaulted on 40 and holds 10.5 scores $\rho = 79\%$ and grades **B**. Without
that term it graded **AAA**, because a write-off removes the asset, releases the provision
and leaves the book reading clean. Separately, a conduct score assesses the manager rather
than the book: the observed default sequence against its best and worst possible orders,
plus a penalty for every loss written off with no prior impairment, and one for every
withdrawal of first-loss capital. See [/mathematics](/mathematics), sections 4 to 6.

## Publish

The reading goes on-chain twice. First as a native XLS-47 Price Oracle: six `PriceData`
entries per vault, one per dimension, with the base asset derived from the vault id and
quote codes `NAV`, `HDL`, `LIQ`, `COV`, `CNC`, `DDL`. An Oracle is a native ledger
object, and `get_aggregate_price` computes a median across independent publishers inside
the ledger, so a second publisher who disagrees with Orma changes the aggregate without
asking. A REST endpoint cannot be contested, only trusted. Second, as a pointer inside the
share token's own XLS-89 metadata, under an `orma` key carrying a `nav_url` template, so a
lender holding nothing but the MPT issuance id can resolve to a valuation without knowing
Orma exists. See [/mathematics](/mathematics), section 8, and
[/nav-pointer](/nav-pointer).

## Enforce

A grade nobody acts on is a press release. The last step makes it bind: Orma issues an
XLS-70 credential of type `ORMA-IG` to an LP, the LP accepts it (issuance alone grants
nothing, a deposit without the accept is still `tecNO_AUTH`), and a vault owner who wants
graded capital names Orma's issuer in a PermissionedDomain and creates the vault with
`tfVaultPrivate`. After that the ledger itself refuses a deposit from an uncredentialed
account. Orma signs nothing in that step, is never asked, and cannot refuse to be cited,
which is the correct shape for a rating. Revocation is asymmetric on purpose: deleting the
credential bounces the next deposit, and leaves the existing position fully withdrawable.
A rater who can trap capital is a worse problem than the one being solved. See
[/gate](/gate).

## Try it

With the backend running (`npm start`, which defaults to port 8787), the valuation route
keyed by share token is the one a third party would call, because the share token id is the
only identifier a holder has. It is one of three routes that opt into a public cache header
for cross-origin callers, alongside the vault-keyed `/api/vaults/{vaultId}/nav` and the
resolver, because all three exist to be called by a party with no relationship to Orma:

```bash
curl -s http://localhost:8787/api/mpt/000000014D667775372D5B78E07FFF294678C7F9CE82AFBC/nav
```

```json
{
  "schema": "orma.nav/1",
  "instrument": {
    "kind": "vault-share",
    "vaultId": "5763707D11EA19D1B5FF04E4EBA4F9336057955CDE65B725D1FF3EF2A96CB0E5",
    "shareMptId": "000000014D667775372D5B78E07FFF294678C7F9CE82AFBC",
    "unitsOutstanding": "51000000",
    "vaultKind": "ClosedEnded",
    "phase": "Investment",
    "redemptionAt": "2026-09-13T15:11:47Z"
  },
  "unitValue": {
    "held": "0.803922",
    "reported": "1.000000",
    "divergenceBps": 1961,
    "basis": "assets net of recognised loss, divided by units outstanding"
  },
  "provenance": {
    "network": "devnet",
    "assetsTotal": "51000000",
    "lossUnrealized": "10000000",
    "unitsOutstanding": "51000000",
    "recompute": "(assetsTotal - lossUnrealized) / unitsOutstanding",
    "buildVersion": "3.4.0-rc5"
  }
}
```

That body is abridged. The full document also carries `serverTime`, `ledgerIndex`, `asOf`,
the asset descriptor inside `instrument`, a `valuation` block and the `assessment`.

Add `?units=1000000` to value a specific pledge, so a lender does not have to reimplement
decimal arithmetic. The captured impairment metadata is served verbatim at
`GET /api/indexer-race`.

<Tip>
  Every monetary quantity on the wire is a decimal string, never a JSON number. XRPL
  `NUMBER` fields carry up to 19 significant digits and `Number()` destroys them silently.
  Orma uses `decimal.js` at 40 digits of precision throughout.
</Tip>

## What this is not

<Note>
  Orma is Devnet software. XLS-65 and XLS-66 are not enabled on Mainnet, so no part of the
  measurement or gating layer can be pointed at production assets today. Two of the three
  rails are exceptions on their own: XLS-47 Price Oracles are live on Mainnet now, and so
  are the XLS-70 credentials and XLS-80 permissioned domains the gate is built from. What
  keeps the gate Devnet-only is the vault underneath it, not the gate.
</Note>

The grade is advisory and is labelled `"advisory": true` inside the `assessment` block of
every response that carries a grade. When the grade cannot be computed the block is `null`
and the valuation stands on its own. Orma is not a regulated rating agency and the method
version is `1.0.0`, which means it has existed for one weekend.

Conduct history is reconstructed from the public transaction record, which a Devnet node
retains for roughly 29 days. Anything a manager did before that window is not visible, and
the API says so in a `caveat` field rather than pretending the record is complete.

Reconstructing that history needed a join that is not documented anywhere: `LoanManage`
carries no `LoanBrokerID` and an impairment does not touch the `LoanBroker` object at all,
so filtering `account_tx` by broker silently drops every impairment.
`Loan.FinalFields.LoanBrokerID` in the same metadata closes it. Before that fix, every
default looked unsignalled, and the rule written to reward early disclosure was punishing
it. That class of mistake is the reason this page leads with an empty `PreviousFields`
rather than with a score.
